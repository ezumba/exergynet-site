Authority & Guardrails

AI Agent
Authorization

An agent that can reason is not the same as an agent that should be trusted to act. ExergyNet separates the two: the model proposes an action, and a separate, deterministic authority layer decides whether it may proceed — so a persuaded or compromised model cannot authorize its own consequences.

The Problem

Reasoning compliance is not authorization

When the same model both decides what to do and grants itself permission to do it, its guardrails are only as strong as its own judgment in the moment — and that judgment can be talked out of position by a clever prompt, poisoned context, or an injection buried in a tool result. If the reasoning engine is the last line of defense before a consequential action, then every jailbreak is also an authorization bypass.

The Approach

A consequence boundary

ExergyNet puts a deterministic authority layer between the agent's proposal and the action it would trigger. The model can propose anything; the boundary evaluates the request against explicit policy, independently of the model's own reasoning, and returns an allow, hold, or reject. Credentials and consequential capabilities live behind that boundary — structurally out of reach of whatever reaches the model.

  • Propose The reasoning engine suggests an action
  • EvaluateA separate, deterministic policy layer judges it — not the model
  • Decide Allow, hold, or reject — with a durable, signed record of the decision
  • Isolate Keys and consequential capabilities never reach the model's context
The Principle

Intelligence and authority are different functions

A reasoning model can fail — be wrong, be jailbroken, be injected — without the authority system failing with it, because authority does not depend on the model's judgment. That separation is the point. It is what lets you give an agent real capability without giving its worst moment real consequences.

Why It Matters More Over Time

From wrong answers to real consequences

For a chatbot, a bad decision is a bad answer. For an agent that moves money, changes records, or controls machines, a bad decision is a transaction or a physical event. As agents move from advice to action, the gap between "the model complied" and "the action was authorized" stops being academic. ExergyNet is built for the side of that line where mistakes have consequences.
