Why This Page Exists

Benchmark the workload. Don't believe the claim.

Every public number on this site is bounded to the envelope it was tested in — a specific hardware target, a specific corpus, a specific baseline. That discipline is exactly why those numbers cannot tell you what ExergyNet will do for your workload, on your infrastructure, against your already-optimized system. The only way to know that is to measure it. This page is the process for doing that.

The Evaluation Process

Six steps. Nothing assumed.

01

Define workload

Name a specific, bounded, state-heavy or multi-step task your organization actually runs today.

02

Freeze success criteria

Agree in advance what counts as a qualified successful task — before any number is measured.

03

Measure current architecture

Baseline your existing, already-optimized system on the frozen criteria. No ExergyNet involved yet.

04

Integrate only what's needed

Add the specific ExergyNet layer relevant to the workload — not the whole architecture by default.

05

Measure again

Re-run the same frozen criteria against the same workload with ExergyNet integrated.

06

Report both outcomes

Positive and negative results are both reported. A workload where ExergyNet doesn't help is a valid, useful outcome.

What Gets Measured

Cost per qualified successful task, itemized where possible.

Where relevant to the workload, the evaluation can account for:

inference retrieval state verification retries orchestration network storage

Not every term is instrumented for every workload today — inference and state overhead are the terms ExergyNet's own published benchmarks currently measure directly (highlighted above). Verification, retries, orchestration, network, and storage costs are accounted for where the specific workload and design partner's own measurement setup make them measurable; this evaluation does not assume they are already solved.
