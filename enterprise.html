The Problem

Increasingly autonomous agents, increasingly fragmented state.

Enterprise AI workflows increasingly span multiple models, multiple sessions, multiple applications, and — increasingly — autonomous agents acting with some degree of independence. Every hand-off between them re-fetches evidence, re-establishes identity, and re-derives what should already be known. None of that cost disappears because the model improved.

Problem 1

Repeated Context Processing

Repeatedly providing large histories to models can create unnecessary token processing and inference overhead. ExergyNet's persistent-state delivery architecture is designed to select relevant evidence without replaying the entire stored corpus. On a tested H200 workload (single NVIDIA H200, Nemotron model, corpus 8,000–285,000 tokens), ExergyNet's xLMP layer delivered approximately 11.3× the correct-task throughput of full-context replay. Those numbers are bounded to that specific test — an enterprise evaluation measures what it means for your own workload.

Capital Efficiency Thesis

Separating persistent-state growth from model-facing context growth.

ExergyNet separates persistent-state growth from model-facing context growth. This creates the possibility of reducing unnecessary repeated inference work and improving accelerator utilization, subject to workload-specific measurement. Capital efficiency is not a fixed number — it is a hypothesis that must be tested against each organization's actual workload.

Institutional knowledge should not have to be replayed through the model every time the organization asks a question. Measuring whether it does — and by how much — is the right starting point.

A100 Evidence: 125× corpus growth, no detected material positive global scaling of mean K

Across 32K–4M nominal stored tokens on 4× A100-SXM4-40GB (TP=4, NVIDIA NIM), a 125× increase in corpus size produced no detected material positive global scaling of mean active model-facing context. Local K upturn from 1M trough through 4M is disclosed and measured. Retrieval work scaled approximately linearly. Any dollar savings implications are to be measured per enterprise workload — no universal cost-reduction figure is offered.

Problem 2

Model-Dependent Operational Memory

When operational knowledge depends entirely on model sessions or model-specific adaptation, changing models can introduce additional migration and continuity requirements. ExergyNet maintains persistent state as a distinct infrastructure concern. Model choice is increasingly a commodity decision — pricing, capability, and vendor relationships change, sometimes quarterly. ExergyNet preserves the externally maintained record of what your organization currently treats as canonical, including provenance, version, authority, permissions, and transition history, independently of any one model's internal representation.

Problem 3

Unbounded Agent Authority

A capable model does not inherently possess permission to execute an action. ExergyNet separates reasoning from policy-governed execution, allowing authority controls to be evaluated independently of model output. The Consequence Boundary places an independent identity, capability, freshness, and integrity check between a model's reasoning and any system that reasoning can affect — constrained only where the applicable enforcement boundary is actually present and active for your deployment, not as a universal guarantee.

Compatibility

Keep your models. Keep your cloud.

ExergyNet is designed to work alongside existing models, applications, and cloud infrastructure. Adopting its state or authority layers does not inherently require replacing an enterprise's model provider or moving every workload to an ExergyNet-hosted environment. The one disclosed exception: Vanguard's OpenAI-compatible API is itself an optional, alternative inference endpoint — it can run alongside your existing provider and is never a requirement of adopting ExergyNet's state or authority layers.

Measure ExergyNet Against Your Own Workload

A design partnership follows a structured measurement process: (1) define a bounded workload with clear performance criteria; (2) measure your current architecture baseline; (3) integrate only the relevant ExergyNet layer; (4) measure again under matched conditions; (5) compare results to your pre-defined criteria; (6) decide whether the evidence justifies adoption. ExergyNet does not ask you to accept benchmark numbers — it asks you to run your own.
