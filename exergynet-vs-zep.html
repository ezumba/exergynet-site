vs. Zep

Both give agents durable memory beyond the context window. Zep models memory as a temporal knowledge graph of evolving facts and relationships; ExergyNet retrieves deterministically by content address and adds a separate authority layer. They are strong at different things.

What Zep Is

Temporal knowledge-graph memory

Zep is agent memory built on Graphiti, its open-source temporal knowledge-graph engine. It represents memory as a "Context Graph" of entities, relationships, and facts, with a bi-temporal model that tracks both when an event occurred and when it was ingested — so facts can be invalidated and superseded over time. Retrieval fuses semantic, full-text, and graph search. Zep is a genuinely strong choice when relationships between entities and how facts change over time are central to your application; Graphiti is open source, with Zep offered as an enterprise-scale service.

The Core Difference

Graph of facts vs. content-addressed evidence

Zep's power is in structure: it extracts entities and relationships into a graph and reasons over how they evolve. ExergyNet takes a different stance — it retrieves committed evidence deterministically by content address, returning the same hash-verifiable bytes for the same query, and treats the authority to act on that evidence as a separate, deterministic decision. Where Zep excels at temporal-relational reasoning, ExergyNet excels at verifiable retrieval and consequence control.

Choosing

When to use which

Choose Zep when…

Your problem is relational and temporal — tracking how entities and facts connect and change over time — and a knowledge-graph model with fact invalidation is exactly the shape you want.

Choose ExergyNet when…

You need retrieval to be deterministic and hash-verifiable, context to stay bounded as the store grows, and a separate authority layer to govern what the agent may act on.
